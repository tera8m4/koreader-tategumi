How each method breaks

1. No rules 禁則なし
Fill the column, break after the last character that fits, then spread the leftover space. Punctuation and closing brackets can land at the top of a column.
2. Push out, like this reader ぶら下げ＋追い出し
Fill the column. If the break lands on a forbidden spot, let a 、 or 。 hang below the column, otherwise push characters back to the next column, then stretch the spacing to fill. This is what processParagraphVertical does.
3. JIS X 4051 / JLReq 追い込み優先
Before stretching, try to pull the next character in by squeezing the half-em spaces that come with 、 」 「 ・. Push out only when there is not enough of that space. No hanging punctuation.
4. BudouX phrases 文節で改行
Break only between the phrases BudouX finds, so no word is split across columns. Columns are left ragged instead of stretched, as with CSS word-break: auto-phrase. A phrase longer than the column is split, keeping word ends together as in 5.
5. Keep BudouX phrases 泣き別れ防止（文節）
Method 3, but each possible break gets a cost: 0 between phrases, 1.5 inside a phrase right before a kanji (飛び|降りる), 3 before a particle (事|が), 8 inside okurigana (飛び降|りる), 20 if fewer than the chosen number of letters stay on one side (沈めてい|く), plus 1 for every em of space the column has to absorb. Squeezing the rest of the phrase in costs 0.3. The cheapest break wins.
6. Keep Yomitan words 泣き別れ防止（単語）
The same rule, but the units are dictionary words found with Yomitan's deinflection rules and your JMdict: 沈めて · いく · と. Breaking between a word and its particle (事|が) is allowed, so columns stay tighter, but a column can start with a lone particle.
7. Keep Yomitan words + particles 泣き別れ防止（単語＋助詞）
The same, with short particles, auxiliaries and the copula (up to 2 letters: が, を, かも, だ) attached to the word before them (事が). This is the proposed default for the reader: no lone mora, no okurigana split and, when it is cheap enough, no lone particle at the top of a column.

The JLReq spacing model

Punctuation glyphs are drawn in half an em; the other half is space that JLReq lets the typesetter squeeze or keep.

Kinsoku rules used here

  • Never at the top of a column (行頭禁則): 、。，．」』）】・：；！？ぁぃぅぇぉっゃゅょァィゥェォッャュョーゝゞ々
  • Never at the bottom of a column (行末禁則): 「『（【〔〈《
  • Never split (分離禁止): ……　――

The adjustment order in panel 3 follows JIS X 4051 and the W3C “Requirements for Japanese Text Layout”: squeeze in first (追い込み), push out only if that fails (追い出し), and stretch what remains evenly.

Simplified model: every kanji and kana is one em, punctuation is half an em plus its space, and there is no ruby, Latin text or hyphenation. The panels show how each rule decides the break; the real renderer measures actual glyph advances.
